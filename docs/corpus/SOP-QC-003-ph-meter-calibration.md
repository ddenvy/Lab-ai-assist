## 1. Purpose

### 1.1 Goal
This procedure describes the two-point calibration of a pH meter before a series of measurements and the criteria for its success.

## 2. Scope

### 2.1 Coverage
It applies to benchtop laboratory pH meters (Mettler Toledo S220) and combination glass electrodes. The procedure is suitable for pH measurements of solutions in the range from 2 to 10.

## 3. Calibration

### 3.1 Buffer solutions
Calibration is performed with pH 4.01 and 6.86 buffers (or 4.01 and 9.18 for the alkaline range). The buffers must differ in temperature from the sample by no more than 5 °C. An opened buffer is valid for 3 months.

### 3.2 Procedure
Rinse the electrode with distilled water, blot it dry without wiping, immerse it in the first buffer, and wait for a stable reading. Repeat for the second buffer. Automatic temperature compensation must be enabled.

## 4. Acceptance Criteria

### 4.1 Electrode slope
The slope after calibration must be from 95% to 102% of the theoretical value.

### 4.2 Check with a control buffer
A check in a pH 9.18 control buffer (when calibrated with 4.01 and 6.86) must give a deviation of no more than ±0.05 pH.

## 5. Measurement Procedure

### 5.1 Sample measurement
The sample is measured at 25 ± 2 °C after rinsing the electrode. The reading is taken after stabilization but no later than 60 seconds. Between samples the electrode is rinsed with distilled water.

## 6. Actions on Non-Conformance

### 6.1 Slope out of range
If the slope is outside the 95–102% range, the electrode is cleaned or replaced and calibration is repeated. If recalibration fails, the pH meter is removed from service and the day's measurements are reviewed.
